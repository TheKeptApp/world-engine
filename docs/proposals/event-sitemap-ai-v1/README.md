# WorldEngine — Event Site-map AI v1

Research cutoff: **7 October 2026**. Recommendation and product scope are analyst judgments. **UNVERIFIED** means public evidence did not establish the claim; it does not mean zero demand or no capability. Vendor AI claims were researched, not tested. No git was used.

## Recommendation

Start with **repeat race-village organizers**, then outdoor festivals/fairs and multi-market managers. Build an import-and-review workflow and natural-language parametric editing on the same scene schema. Sell faster layout revision, supplier coordination and a useful guest view. Keep novel AI-made objects optional until dimension, style, licensing and review costs are demonstrated.

AI layout is already competitive territory. EventDiagram advertises prompt-based editable layouts and plan import; Cvent advertises prompt-to-diagram and banquet-order extraction. Ticket Fairy advertises rule-based AI trader allocation into existing pitches. The opportunity is a carefully executed combination of outdoor alignment, editable true-scale kit objects, provenance, date-specific sun/shade and shareable 3D—not a defensible claim to being first. The full combined workflow was not verified in an incumbent; this is an evidence limit, not proof of absence. [EventDiagram](https://eventdiagram.com/features/), [Cvent](https://www.cvent.com/en/supplier-venue/event-diagramming-software), [Ticket Fairy pitch rules](https://www.ticketfairy.com/guides/set-pitch-rules-and-let-fai-plan-your-trader-pitches)

The existing Creator Kit v2 prototype provides interaction context; its place, AI and weather behaviours do not establish production feasibility or demand. Keep the approved Style B geometry/material standard and the protected base place separate from editable organizer overlays.

## Segment ranking and market size

| Rank | Initial buyer and job | Why this order | Principal uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Race directors/timers managing repeated villages, aid stations and finish chutes | Repeated kit objects, route-relative language, operational and guest-map need | Paid demand; do not replace course certification |
| 2 | Outdoor festival/fair production teams | Complex temporary layouts and frequent revisions | Incumbent GIS/CAD workflows and professional requirements |
| 3 | Multi-market managers | Reusable pitches, vendor rotation and sponsor inventory | Small budgets; simpler 2D tools may suffice |
| 4 | Outdoor wedding venues/rental firms | Repeated site plus rain alternatives; curated rental inventory | One-off couples are inefficient customers; strong venue tools |
| 5 | Street-fair/BID organizers | Booth, entrance and street-layout coordination | Permit/traffic expertise and festival overlap |
| 6 | Campus events/facilities teams | Reusable real-place base across event types | Procurement and ownership fragmented across departments |
| 7 | Temporary outdoor concert producers | Stages, access, utilities and supplier coordination | High specialist needs; fixed-venue shows add few new layouts |
| 8 | Tournament/fan-zone operators | Temporary overlays and attendee directions | Broad segment; stadium/sports CAD is outside initial scope |

This order is a product/distribution hypothesis, not an empirical attractiveness score. The economically useful unit is a **repeat organizer account and a new or changed layout**, not an attendee, wedding registration or recurring market day.

Current US annual applicable-event counts were **not verified** for these segments. Useful evidence exists, but has incompatible denominators:

- RunSignup's 2025 dataset contains **97,127 distance/event options** and 12.19 million registrations, not 97,127 unique race weekends or site plans. Registration market share cannot scale the option count. [RaceTrends report](https://info.runsignup.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/02/25-Race-Trends-FOR-ONLINE-compressed-1.pdf)
- FestivalNet advertises **more than 26,000 mixed US/Canada listings**, not a national annual US festival census. [Directory](https://festivalnet.com/)
- USDA estimated **8,140 operating markets in 2019** across the lower 48; this is historical recurring market locations, not 2026 sessions. [USDA survey](https://www.ams.usda.gov/services/local-regional/research-publications/fmms)
- Live Nation reported **34,784 North American events in 2025**; one company, US plus Canada, including fixed venues. [2025 filing](https://investors.livenationentertainment.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001335258-26-000009/lyv-20251231.htm)
- NCES counted **3,722 degree-granting institutions in 2022–23**, not annual campus events. NCAA's **92 championship programs** are not tournament sites or all sports games. [NCES](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/d23/tables/dt23_317.20.asp), [NCAA](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-ncaa-changes-awards-name-to-elite-scholar-athlete-from-elite-90/)
- CDC recorded **2,128,446 provisional marriages in 2024**, including ceremonies without outdoor receptions. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/dvs/marriage-divorce/national-marriage-divorce-rates-00-24.pdf)

Do not add these figures into a TAM. Festivals, concerts and street fairs overlap; campus sports overlap other segments. Sports tourism expenditure is not organizer spend. Illustrative sensitivity calculations only: 8,140 markets × an **UNVERIFIED** 20–40 annual sessions = 162,800–325,600 event-days; 3,722 institutions × an **UNVERIFIED** 5–20 relevant events = 18,610–74,440 editions; marriages × an **UNVERIFIED** 10–25% relevant outdoor share ≈212,845–532,112 editions. These are not demand estimates, current counts or distinct buyers.

Build a bottom-up market model from named organizer accounts, series counts, layout changes per year, qualifying input files, current mapping cost and paid conversion. Municipal permits can support local evidence; extrapolating one city nationally would be unreliable.

## Budgets, map makers and today's sharing

No defensible national typical production or mapping-software budget was verified for races, festivals, fairs, concerts, sports or campuses. USDA's historical market survey found an average **$33,222 annual/seasonal operating budget among the 48% reporting one**. The Knot's commercial 2026 study reported 2025 wedding spending of **$34,000 mean / $24,000 median**, from 10,474 membership-recruited couples; this is ceremony/reception spending, not a venue software budget. [USDA budget report](https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/GeneralCharacteristicsandMarketPractices.pdf), [Wedding study](https://images.theknot.com/rules/2026/the-knot-real-weddings-study-data-read-out-2026.pdf)

Organizer maps range from simple drawings to professional plans. Austin's permit examples require overhead equipment/site details; Seattle explicitly accepts hand drawings or a map image edited in Paint. These establish input diversity. They do not establish the national share of Canva, Illustrator, CAD, OnePlan or Social Tables. [Austin site-plan examples](https://www.austintexas.gov/sites/default/files/files/ACE/Site_Plan_Examples_2026.pdf), [Seattle paperwork](https://www.seattle.gov/documents/departments/arts/downloads/grants/putartsparks/aip%20parks%20paperwork%20tip%20sheet-%20events.pdf)

Likely creators are race directors/timers, production designers, venue planners, rental firms, market managers and municipal/campus facilities staff; tool mappings in the CSV are explicitly hypotheses. Permit PDFs/images, crew/vendor plans, public website/mobile maps and print/QR are separate outputs. Permit submission and RunSignup web/RaceJoy map channels are documented; email/print prevalence is unmeasured. [RunSignup sponsor/map channels](https://info.runsignup.com/use-cases/sponsor-management/)

Interview 15–25 organizers and request actual maps, revisions, supplier inventories, budgets, mapping bills and staff hours. Proposed willingness-to-pay tests—**not observed budgets**—are $25–100/month for a small repeat operator, $100–500/month for a promoter/venue and $5,000–25,000/year for enterprise service. The prior $149/event publish idea remains an unvalidated test. Compare per-event and series subscriptions in paid pilots rather than adopt these numbers as prices.

## V1 feature scope

| Ship in the core pilot | Restricted pilot / next | Defer |
|---|---|---|
| PDF and image upload, page/crop, source overlay; clean DXF with units/layers | Phone-photo rectification with explicit low-confidence review | Universal DWG/CAD conversion |
| Two-point similarity alignment plus independent check point; optional multi-point affine | Auto-suggest anchors with visible evidence | Unreviewed auto-georeferencing |
| Finite taxonomy: tents, stages, tables, toilets, barriers, gates, signs and route lines; unresolved tray | Ambiguous legends and unfamiliar supplier symbols | Unattended semantic conversion |
| Source crop, class/dimension provenance and per-item approve/change/dismiss | Deliberate bulk approval after inspecting a homogeneous group | Automatic publish or hidden omissions |
| Typed parametric create/move/rotate/array commands, named areas/entrances and route chainage | Weather alternatives and reviewed shadow-based placement | Automated shelter/safety certification |
| Curated Style B kit with supplier-size presets, metric geometry and service envelopes | Optional AI-made decor/booth shells with rights and style review | Generated structural stage/stairs/barrier substitutes |
| Undo/version history, private operational layers, public guest view, dated 2D export | Sponsor review link and limited inventory/status | Automated sponsor pricing/ROI or crowd simulation claims |

The catalogue contains **40 proposed families**, not an empirical top-40 popularity ranking. P0/P1 are product priorities. Listed dimensions are modelling assumptions; supplier modules and operational requirements take precedence. Stage parameters include width/depth/riser/stairs/roof/backdrop; assembly presets should constrain valid combinations. Parts may carry clearance, queue and utility envelopes separately from visible meshes.

### End-to-end flow

1. Choose the real place and event edition/date. Upload a plan; retain an immutable original and select its page/crop.
2. Confirm scale, units, geographic north and matching anchors. Rectify photos first; treat unscaled sketches as hints, then request dimensions. A third point verifies a two-point fit; three affine anchors alone do not independently prove accuracy.
3. Detect finite classes/labels/routes and build a highlighted private draft. Show uncertain or unresolved symbols; never quietly discard them.
4. Review each proposal against its source crop and dimensional origin. Accept, replace kit family, resize, move or dismiss. Preserve source and decision history.
5. Edit by plain language: “stage on the north side, 3 ft riser” becomes a typed stage proposal with exact 0.9144 m riser, visible assumptions for missing size and a geographic boundary anchor. “At mile 2” uses the chosen route's chainage; “every 50 m” exposes start/end, side and setback.
6. Preview date-specific sun and timestamped weather; compare a rain scenario. Organizer/suppliers approve final practical arrangements.
7. Publish the approved edition to guest or sponsor audiences, with private layers excluded. Guests see directions, POIs and schedule. Export a dated plan for coordination; the output is not automatic permit approval.

## Competitive pricing and differentiation

Examples checked at the cutoff: OnePlan advertises Pro **$99/month or $984/year** and a limited free plan; Cvent's diagramming tiers start at **$49/month or $480/year**, with annual commitment; EventDiagram advertises **$18/month or $180/year**. Product scope, commitments and seat limits differ. OnePlan and SeatPlan have conflicting indexed/legacy price pages; verify current checkout. [OnePlan pricing](https://www.oneplan.io/pricing/), [Cvent pricing](https://www.cvent.com/en/event-marketing-management/cvent-event-design-software/pricing), [EventDiagram](https://eventdiagram.com/features/)

Treat overlay import, editable drawing extraction, indoor seating recognition, outdoor pitch allocation and true 3D object creation as different capabilities. A BEO parser is not georeferenced image recognition. A plan-background upload is not semantic import. Prismm/Allseated is transitioning to Cvent and should not be counted as two independent current competitors. Full details and limitations are in the competitor table.

Defensibility would come from a rights-cleared event-plan evaluation set, supplier-aware parametric kit, persistent real-place anchors, visible uncertainty, review history and practical coordination integrations. Realistic rendering alone does not establish paid demand.

## Sponsor activation

Sponsors buy bundled rights, exposure and fulfillment. A booth's proximity to entrance, stage or finish can matter, alongside exclusivity, signage, digital promotion, tickets and hospitality. Local examples: Lenexa's 2026 presenting festival package is **$5,000 with a 20×20 ft booth and promotional benefits**; its platinum package is **$815 with 10×10 ft booth plus promotion**. A World A'Fair's 2025 **$7,500 gold tier** bundles an entrance tent/booth with other benefits. These are package examples, not national booth rents or objective location valuations. [Lenexa](https://www.lenexa.com/Events-Activities/Get-Involved/Sponsorship), [A World A'Fair](https://www.aworldafair.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/AWorld-AFair-Sponsorship-Information-2025-Festival.pdf)

Proposed sponsor flow: reserve a slot → attach contracted deliverables/category exclusivity → compare placements and rain alternatives → sponsor reviews a restricted link → organizer approves/locks → capture onsite proof → report measured fulfillment. Connect a spatial record to the organizer's sponsor ledger rather than build billing in v1. RunSignup already supports tiers, benefits and commitment/payment tracking. [Sponsor management](https://info.runsignup.com/2026/03/12/race-sponsors/)

The sponsor view should show footprint dimensions, available/reserved/approved status, audience-facing camera, relationship to paths/entrances/stages, neighbor categories, utilities, loading windows, date-specific shade, timestamped forecast, alternative layout, comments and a dated downloadable plan. Redact sensitive operations and unrelated commercial terms. Value discussion should use agreed attendance, observed counts, consented lead scans and fulfilled contracted benefits. Map views or simulated footfall do not prove impressions, conversions or ROI.

## Validation and go/no-go

Obtain 30–50 rights-cleared plans from several organizers, holding out organizers and drawing styles. Compare manual rebuild with assisted rebuild, separately for vector PDF, raster, DXF and real phone photos. Measure class precision/recall, labels, dimensions, positions, route continuity, independent alignment error, unresolved objects, correction time and final false accepted objects. There is no verified outdoor event-plan accuracy figure in this research.

Proposed gates are **targets, not results**: at least 50% faster completion in the first segment; ≥95% precision for high-confidence core-class proposals; every uncertain scale visible; no unknown symbol silently omitted; full reversibility. Add paid repeat usage and guest/supplier usefulness as business gates. A technically successful importer without organizer willingness to pay is insufficient.

Generated 3D has low raw API task costs in some services, but reviewed asset cost and Style B success rate remain unverified. Exact dimensions, usable hidden geometry and licensing are separate checks. See feasibility for current model/API comparisons and human approval boundaries. The importer remains useful if generated assets are disabled.

Research gaps to resolve before claiming market size or launch readiness: unique US layout counts, mapping-tool prevalence, actual organizer/software budgets, import accuracy and review cost, realistic browser performance, map/weather data rights, generation SaaS rights, and sponsor willingness to pay for the view.
