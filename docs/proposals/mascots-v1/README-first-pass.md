# WorldEngine mascot exploration v1
7 October 2026 · Style B: rich stylized, warm and vibrant · Eight original authored directions

## Recommendation
**1. Nimbindle — best WorldEngine parent mascot.** The curled coral ribbon, open gap and teal underside make a strong silhouette with a compact colour signature. Its weather-responsive posture fits the living world without tying the engine to dogs, jobs or one forecast symbol. It can be lively in a game and quiet during a walk. Main risk: abstract form needs a disciplined turnaround/rig so the curl does not become an arbitrary cloud.

**2. Stoopkin — most distinctive neighbourhood identity.** The stepped terracotta body and turquoise canopy connect directly to homes, welcome and small local jobs. It survives a small icon better than an accessory-dependent humanoid and feels native to a block. It is especially strong for the jobs game and home-centred weather. Main risk: keep it welcoming and mobile; the deliberately architectural form should not look like a stationary piece of street furniture.

**3. Curbpip — strongest resident-of-the-street character.** A pigeon belongs naturally near transit, sidewalks and ordinary neighbourhood activity. The asymmetric shoulder bib, compact pear body and three-part tail provide a clear local identity. It is an approachable game/world ambassador with simple movement. Main risk: bird mascots are a crowded visual category, and the indigo body needs a light or warm contrasting surround at small sizes.

Boughbit is the best DogWell specialist; Rainwispel is the best rain/wetness specialist; Waynookle is the strongest explicit guide. Orbituft is a good quiet sky companion. Daywhorl is immediately weather-readable, but its sun/cloud category is the least distinctive parent-brand direction.

## What is included
Each numbered PNG contains **14 studies**: front/side/back, five expressions, rain/snow/sun/night, one small street-world appearance and a flat icon treatment. Eight separate numbered `-icon.svg` files are original vector simplifications for app-icon evaluation. `index.html` presents all eight icons at 32/64/128 layout pixels, street-panel crops up to 390 px wide, and expandable full sheets. `image-prompts.json` preserves all generation prompts, the Rainwispel correction, name-screen notes and file hashes.

The PNG studies used the built-in image generator. The standalone SVG icons are newly authored vector sketches, not extracted or traced bitmap crops; they intentionally simplify face, feet and body details. They are exploration assets, not approved production marks or final platform exports. Rounded icon backgrounds are presentation treatments; production exports should use the platform's required full canvas and masking.

Style reference viewed: `Desktop/world-engine/docs/proposals/look-fix-v1/images/lighting-03-ordinary-1530.png`. Read-only context: vegetation-v1 and live-world-v1 README files. User's Style B definition controls: simplified 3D forms, warm saturated colour, bright highlights, deep coloured shade, soft long shadows in low sun and atmospheric distance. No photo textures, individual fur/feathers, bricks or grass blades in production assets.

These are newly authored designs with no intentional borrowing of existing mascot costumes, faces, signature props or brand assets. They have not been exhaustively cleared against all existing character/IP libraries. The requests for originality and name checking are addressed by distinct design construction and a preliminary screen; final commercial selection still **needs a legal check**.

## Eight directions

### 1. Nimbindle — Weather ribbon spirit
![Nimbindle character sheet](01-nimbindle-sheet.png)

**Name ideas:** Nimbindle; Curlmori. **Needs a legal check.** No obvious exact-name matches surfaced for either candidate.

**Best fit:** WorldEngine parent mascot; block-weather app; gentle DogWell weather companion.

Nimbindle notices changes before it speaks: a breeze through a porch, a brighter patch of pavement, the first drops on a leaf. It is attentive, quietly playful and happy to accompany a walk without turning every moment into a task. Its ribbon curl opens when conditions are inviting and gathers inward when shelter would be welcome. It explains uncertainty plainly and never pretends its expression is a weather measurement. Across apps, it is the same observant neighbour rather than a different costumed employee.

**Design and turnaround:** A small floating folded ribbon of warm coral weather, shaped like an asymmetric curled comma with an open triangular negative-space window under a swept-back three-lobed vapor crest. A compact apricot oval face inset on the inside of the curl, two dark horizontal seed-shaped eyes, tiny plum mouth, two short mitten-like fin hands. NO white generic cloud head, no star-shaped body, no lightning bolts, no clothing. Back is a continuous coral ribbon with one broad teal underside stripe. Unique curled ribbon silhouette must persist in all views.

**Five expressions:** joy, curious, concerned, determined and sleepy. Joy opens the pose and eyes into broad arcs; curiosity uses a head/body tilt and one clear small mouth; concern lowers the eye corners and gathers the pose; determination narrows the eyes and leans forward; sleep closes the eyes and compresses the posture. These five remain recognizable through pose when the face is too small.

**Weather responses:** Rain: crest leans forward like a shelter, small blue drops around it, quietly attentive. Snow: broad white cap on top curl, arms tucked in, delighted. Sun: crescent body opens in a relaxed stretch with bright edge highlight. Night: same coral silhouette under cool fill, apricot face softly lit, not a neon ghost.

**Small world appearance:** 0.45 m tall, floating 0.15 m above a front-yard bed near sidewalk, tiny beside the house and trees. Proposed longest dimension is 0.45 m. At 8 m with a 390 px-wide, 60° horizontal camera, this is approximately 19.0 layout pixels; at 20 m it is 7.6 px. Read silhouette and colour at those sizes; do not expect expression detail. Generated world-panel framing is illustrative, not calibrated.

**Flat icon:** flat coral comma ribbon with triangular gap, apricot face and two plum seed eyes against deep teal. [Separate scalable icon](01-nimbindle-icon.svg). Keep the silhouette and two dominant body colours at 32 px; eyes are secondary. Simplifications in the vector sketch are intentional and should be reconciled with the chosen 3D model during final design.

**Design discipline:** Strongest parent-brand candidate. Recognize the coral curl, open gap and teal underside before the face. Keep the open gap large in the icon. Avoid turning crest into a generic cloud.

## Small-scale 3D and icon rules
All measurements and thresholds here are authored starting values, not phone test results. Model the mascot at its proposed physical size. Give grounded characters proper contact, and floating spirits consistent hover clearance. Never enlarge a mascot to dog/human scale merely to retain a face at distance. Do not change WorldEngine's mapped buildings, paths, transit, solar position or weather to suit a vignette.

At a 390 layout px viewport and 60° horizontal FOV, centre-frame projected size = **337.75 × size_m / depth_m**. Drawable pixels equal layout pixels times device scale. A 0.35 m mascot is 29.6 / 14.8 / 5.9 px at 4 / 8 / 20 m. At normal street distances, its broad shape carries the identity, not tiny pupils or props.

- **Over 32 layout px:** full silhouette, broad face and large gesture; no fine texture.
- **16–32 px:** simplify to primary body/tail/crest/canopy, retain two dominant colour areas, eyes only if they resolve.
- **8–16 px:** shape and colour; remove brows, cheek wedge, nose highlights and finger/foot subdivisions.
- **Below 8 px:** optional very simple silhouette if still useful; otherwise fade/hide the decorative character. A selected character may have a separate clearly presented portrait in the UI.
- Do not add a glow or map pin to every mascot. Placement, silhouette and local background contrast should do the work. Preserve actual physical scale for distant characters.
- App icons at 32 px retain the large asymmetric signature; 64 px may show eyes; 128 px can show nose/tab. No letters, small scenery or weather symbols inside the icon.
- Initial near-mesh target: roughly 600–1,200 triangles, medium 150–350, far 40–100; shared opaque materials and limited rig parts. These are design budgets inside existing scene ceilings, not measured performance.
- Body corners: 1–3% of member width in broad fillets, with smooth rounded organic body masses and planar architectural faces for Stoopkin. Avoid featureless blobs or plush fur shaders. Two/three broad material areas plus readable light are enough.
- Animation: one distinct idle, one arrival, one pointing/acknowledging gesture and four weather poses. Keep weather reactions slow and sparse; reduced motion uses still poses. Avoid constant bouncing.
- Weather response is presentation driven by environment state. A wet/snowy mascot is not proof of a measured local event. Preserve source/freshness/uncertainty in explicit app information.

DogWell should keep the character supportive: no guilt for missed walks, unsafe-weather encouragement or cues telling a dog to chase a critter. Jobs helpers should describe tasks without appearing to authorize private-property access. These are personality/product choices, not additional mechanics.

## Review, authority and remaining work
All eight selected PNG sheets were visually inspected for 3 turnarounds, 5 expression cells, 4 weather cells, a small world placement and a 2D icon. Rainwispel's first joy pose incorrectly added a third bowl stalk; the selected sheet corrects it to two. SVGs are flat, parseable vector illustrations with broad shapes. The review page shows icon sizes and source world-panel crops without changing generated image pixels.

The artwork remains exploratory: sheets are not guaranteed orthographic measurements, rig specifications, live observations or projection-calibrated phone captures. Expression poses can rotate a character; side views use readable profiles. Incidental grass, plant/ground detail, minor surface grain, exaggerated sky symbols and one background vehicle in generated vignettes do not belong in the production mascot/world design. Use the large masses, warm/cool light and numeric scale guidance. The small world vignettes are illustrative invented blocks, not specific mapped real addresses.

Front/side/back identity is suitable for concept selection; final modelling must lock thickness, asymmetry and attachment points, especially Nimbindle's curl, Waynookle's folds, Rainwispel's two bowls and Orbituft's open collar. Fine icon/3D differences should be resolved after selecting a direction rather than turning all eight into production assets.
