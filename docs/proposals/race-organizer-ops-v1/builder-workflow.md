# Builder AI workflow
A: proposed algorithm, checked 2026-10-07.

1. Load the latest closure map, survey and permitted polygon; attach timestamps and jurisdiction IDs. Missing inputs yield a pending proposal, never approval.
2. Protect existing water, habitat, roots, public/cycle access, utilities and prohibited areas. Do not invent wider paths or new pavement.
3. Build independent runner, public/accessible, vendor/service and EMS connectivity graphs. Any uncontrolled crossing, disconnected service or dead-end emergency approach is a blocking issue.
4. Reserve medical/evacuation, finish runoff and start holding before sanitation, registration, gear, food, expo and stage. Use full footprints including ballast, guys and queue envelopes.
5. Recompute counts using total users, duration and peak demand. Accessible sanitation is checked per cluster. A site that cannot hold the reservations is reduced or relocated.
6. Test start waves against the narrowest usable course segment and overlapping runners. Initial flow coefficients are assumptions; crowd review and marshal confirmation determine releases.
7. Verify path distance separately from certification. Keep measured start, finish, turn and barrier coordinates immutable once certified.
8. Produce an issue report with each rule's status, evidence, actual value, applicable threshold, dependencies and reviewer. Numeric unknowns remain null.
9. Require qualified medical, accessibility, fire/EMS, traffic/crowd and permitting review. Record their conditions with dates. Human approvals govern event operation and weather decisions.
10. Recheck after weather, closure, vendor or geometry changes. On event day keep services until course reconciliation and sweep are complete.

Model geometry or generated pictures never supply missing measured data. A generic preset is neither a medical staffing plan nor crowd-capacity certification.
