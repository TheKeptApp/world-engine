# Research + Builder AI operating rules
Checked 2026-10-07. **V** supports the cited published statement in its scope. **A** is a proposed planning default. **U** needs verification. This is not an approved event plan.

## Typical layouts [A unless explicitly sourced]
| Race | Course pattern | Village and operational emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| 5K | Single loop or out-and-back, broad start leading to narrower course | Compact registration, start water, medical and clear finish; short event still needs sanitation and weather plan |
| 10K | Loop/out-and-back; separate opposing flows | Multiple water opportunities, split/turnaround control, extended finish spread |
| Half | One loop, point-to-point or controlled multi-loop | More aid, fueling, toilets on course, bag transport if different start/finish, late-runner support |
| Marathon | Loop or point-to-point with documented crossings | Extended medical/communications coverage, transit disruption plan, multiple aid/toilet sites and accessible participant support |
| Trail | Terrain-specific loops/point-to-point | Small waves before singletrack, time-based aid gaps, extraction/access map, swept checkpoints, protected habitat |
| Triathlon | Swim → controlled transition → bike → transition → run | Water rescue, water-quality/weather decisions, rack security, separate leg flows, mount/dismount lines |
| Obstacle | Running links between engineered obstacles | Queue/bypass/rescue area at every obstacle; falling zone and structural approval; risk-specific medical coverage |

**V:** USA Triathlon resources describe course/logistics/waves and transition flow; mount/dismount boundaries matter. [USA Triathlon operations](https://www.usatriathlon.org/race-director/resources/event-operations-and-logistics); [USA Triathlon transition guide](https://www.usatriathlon.org/articles/training-tips/eight-tips-for-a-quick-smooth-transition).
**V:** World Obstacle's 2023 medical rules include medical leadership and evacuation provisions for their governed events. **A:** AI must load applicable federation requirements rather than reuse a road-race staffing ratio. Full applicability and current local event staffing remain U. [World Obstacle medical rules2023](https://www.worldobstacle.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/FISO-Medical-Rules.pdf); [World Obstacle medical](https://worldobstacle.org/medical/).

## Corrals, waves and timing [A]
No universal verified “width per runner,” safe density or wave interval was found. Start with **0.75 m per illustrative runner column** and **0.75 m² net holding area per staged runner**, plus independent aisles, exits and wheelchair accommodation. Neither number is a certified capacity. Never pack everyone into a narrow public path because a rectangle fits.

Calculate a provisional release-time check:
`wave clearance = wave runners / (minimum effective width × assumed release flow) + clearance buffer`.
Illustrative release flow **0.8 runners/s/m**, buffer **60 s**; actual bottlenecks, acceleration, opposing flows, wheelchairs, turns and surface conditions require field simulation and crowd review. Downstream clear confirmation overrides a timer. When the required width is unavailable, reduce wave size, relocate or reject; never widen real paths in the map.

Timing mats span the approved width with low-profile edges and protected cables. Place vendor electronics away from queues; obtain read-zone/antenna separation from the timing vendor, not a generic rule. Include backup timing/video and split verification appropriate to distance. Cones/barriers must reproduce certified restrictions. Start and finish can share infrastructure only when time-separated and verified to avoid opposing flows.

## Finish and medical [A]
Provisional clear chute lengths: **50/80/150 m** for 500/2,000/10,000 runners. Preserve the first **30 m** for slowing down and moving onward; medals, photographers and food go farther downstream in widening dispersal space. Medical access is immediately to the side, with a provisional tent target within **20 m** of finish runoff, visible to staff and connected to EMS access. No forced turn into a queue.

**V:** RRCA calls for on-site medical support regardless of distance/size, coordinated communications, and aid remaining open until the course clears. **A:** medical tents shown here are footprint reservations, not adequate staffing. Medical lead owns AED reach, response time, ambulances, cooling, treatment privacy and hospital arrangements. [RRCA safe-event guidelines](https://www.rrca.org/education/event-directors/safe-event-guidelines/).

## Toilets, aid and support
**V:** sanitation guidance accounts for synchronized race demand and total attendance. Welsh Athletics gives 1/30 unisex units, or 1/50 with additional urinal provision; DMSE discusses generous 1/50 versus a 1/100 lower bound. These are not equivalent US legal mandates. [PSAI event sanitation](https://psai.org/renting-portable-units/); [Welsh Athletics toilet fact sheet](https://www.welshathletics.org/image/Documents/Licences/Licensing%20Portal%20docs/Race%20Director%20resources%20with%20WA%20logo/race-director-fact-sheet-number-2-toilet-provision.pdf); [DMSE race toilet discussion](https://dmse-sports.squarespace.com/press-coverage/everything-race-directors-need-to-know-about-port-a-potties).
**A:** use **ceil(runners/50)** as an initial no-urinal planning count: **10,40,200**. Test peak queues; consider 1/30 and add spectators/staff, course sites, duration and servicing. Count reliable permitted permanent facilities only after confirming hours/stock and queue capacity. Place on level serviceable ground, with washing, waste and delivery access; keep queues off courses and medical lanes.

**V:** ADA portable-cluster provisions and route standards apply where scoped. **A:** reserve at least one accessible unit and round 5% upward **per cluster**, not venue-wide. Accessible units count within total. Example clusters 10;20+20;50+50+50+50 require pack reservations of **1,2,12** accessible units. ADA walking routes require 36-inch minimum width, firm stable slip-resistant surface, maximum 1:20 running slope and 1:48 cross-slope; turns, ramps, toilet maneuvering, counters and arrival access need full compliance review. **A:** prefer 1.8 m village routes, not the bare minimum. Race-course participation access is a separate assessment. [ADA Standards](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/).

**V:** historical World Athletics road guidance places water at roughly 5 km intervals, more frequently when conditions warrant. This is not a universal current local requirement. [World Athletics road-running manual](https://worldathletics.org/download/download?filename=1d445793-24b4-4821-98e4-38fc55b9f8ef.pdf&urlslug=IAAF+Road+Running+Manual).
**A:** 5K: start/finish plus near 2.5 km; 10K: near 2.5/5/7.5 km; half/marathon: target 2.5 km gaps, medically revise for exposure and time on feet. Trail: initial maximum 30-minute slow-participant gap, subject to carried supplies, terrain and extraction. These are opportunities to drink, not prescribed individual consumption. Medical/nutrition lead decides volumes and electrolytes; the AI must not prescribe forced fluid intake.

**A:** aid setup: warning about 100 m before; tables outside the racing envelope with a volunteer working strip; no station on a bend, bridge, downhill brake point or turnaround. Separate water/other offerings, leave bypass space and roughly 50 m downstream cleanup. Bin positions cannot narrow measured course. Service replenishment avoids live crossings.

**A:** gear check uses numbered retrieval, weather protection and separate inbound/outbound queues; expo is optional and downstream of dispersal. Stage/PA faces spectators, not medical care or neighboring homes, with permitted sound levels and backup announcement channels. Food requires authority approvals, potable water, waste, fire review and separation from sanitation—15 m here is a planning buffer, not health code.

## Tents, barriers and travel [A]
Common modules: 10×10 ft (3.048×3.048 m), 10×20 ft (3.048×6.096 m), 20×20 ft (6.096×6.096 m). Dimensions alone omit ballast, guys, fire separation and queues. Tent spacing, anchorage, wind limits, stage loads, cooking/generator separation and extinguisher requirements come from supplier/authority. No universal fire gap is approved here.

Pedestrian rail channels people, with feet clear of running space and staffed escape gates. Cones mark a route; they do not protect against vehicles. Water-filled barriers are not automatically crash-rated. Street closures and hostile-vehicle mitigation need approved traffic/engineering plans.

**V:** Denver's published 2024 fire guideline references 20 ft adjacent street emergency clearance unless the fire permit specifies otherwise. Its current application needs reconfirmation. **A:** reserve 6.1 m service road space provisionally; additionally verify turning, overhead clearance, load support, gates and hydrants. Do not impose that vehicle lane on a narrow trail by inventing pavement. [Denver Fire 2024 guidelines](https://denver.prelive.opencities.com/files/assets/public/v/2/fire-department/documents/dfd-special-events-guidelines-2024.pdf).

**A:** calculate parking from permitted stalls and arrival demand, not event headcount alone. For initial modeling only: 0.6 runner auto-arrival share and 2.0 runners/car produce 150/600/3,000 runner cars, before staff/spectators. Verify transit schedule and accessibility; shuttle loading needs separate pedestrian/vehicle space. A nominal 40 seated passengers per bus trip is an assumption, not vehicle capacity. If capacity fails, use remote permitted parking/transit or reduce attendance. Never appropriate neighborhood/private parking.

## Permits — local examples
**V:** Denver's published timeline lists park/OSE/closure applications at least 60 days ahead, with medical/emergency/fire and other tasks around 30 days and completion before setup. 2025 park form also states 60 days for public events. **U:** 2026 exact booking windows, Sloan's permitted lawn boundaries/blackouts, temporary structures and all case-specific requirements. **A:** start 120–180 days ahead and reconfirm deadline measured against setup. [Denver event timeline](https://www.denvergov.org/files/assets/public/v/1/special-events/documents/event-application-timeline.pdf); [Denver 2025 park application](https://denvergov.org/files/assets/public/v/3/parks-and-recreation/documents/rentals-amp-permits/2025_parkpermitapplication.pdf).

**V:** Chicago Park District requires permits for routes, tents, sponsorship, sound and other triggers, a site plan and step-off route map, insurance and at least 14 days review. Extra city permits may be triggered by street closures, food, sales/alcohol, tents above 400 ft² or stages above 2 ft. The city ordinance has a 21-day application floor; the 2025 guide favors submissions above 60 days. **A:** plan 120–180 days, not 14 or 21; **U:** current 2026 case-specific deadlines and park/city approvals. [Chicago Park permits](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/permits-rentals-101); [Chicago special-event ordinance](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/chicago/latest/chicago_il/0-0-0-2650379); [Chicago2025 resourceguide](https://ipi.cityofchicago.org/Resources/Information/DCASE/Special_Events_Resource_Guide.pdf).

**V:** Greenville **SC** designates Permit B for races/walks/parades. **U:** current B lead time was not present in retrieved page. An older city Permit A page states 90 days with roads/60 without, but that cannot be presented as the B deadline. Confirm trail manager, parks reservations, insurance, traffic/police, sound, temporary structures and food approvals. County trail commercial advertising requires authorization. **A:** hold 120 days in the workback plan while actual deadline remains pending. Never substitute Greenville NC rules. [GreenvilleSC permittypes](https://www.greenvillesc.gov/permits-for-parades-festivals-parties-athletic-events); [GreenvilleSC PermitB page](https://www.greenvillesc.gov/1796/Permit-B---Races-Walks-Parades); [GreenvilleSC PermitA archivedportal](https://connections.greenvillesc.gov/1795/Permit-A); [GreenvilleCounty trail ordinance](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/greenvillecounty/latest/greenvilleco_sc/0-0-0-6067).

## Certification [V, with A implementation]
USATF course certification is distance accuracy; event sanction is separate. Calibrated-bicycle measurement follows the shortest permitted route with pre/post calibration and short-course prevention factor. The referenced RRTC manual uses 1.001. Barrier/turnaround/start/finish locations must match the measured map. **A:** map polyline/GPS length is only a proposal estimate; do not label these concepts certified. Recheck course status after any reroute. Certification does not authorize road closure or certify crowd safety. [USATF certification](https://www.usatf.org/resources/course-certification); [USATF RRTC measurement manual](https://www.rrtc.net/procedures_manual/html_version/Version-2019-1-001/RRTC/RRTC_Procedures_Manual_Version-2019-1-001.pdf).

## Heat, lightning and emergency decisions
**V:** RRCA's page combines an EAS flag table (green through29.3°C) with separate modify/cancel guidance around82°F WBGT; a historical IAAF table sets black above28°C. This conflict is material. The World Athletics index lists newer medical/heat documents, but linked Beat the Heat PDF was inaccessible. **U:** an appropriate current adopted numeric policy for these races. **A:** ship null thresholds, requiring medical director approval, sport/climate/acclimatization context, calibrated on-site outdoor WBGT and a responsible monitor. Flags mean green monitor, yellow caution, red high-risk intervention and black stop; color alone cannot grant permission. [RRCA safe-event guidelines](https://www.rrca.org/education/event-directors/safe-event-guidelines/); [IAAF 2013 medical guidelines](https://worldathletics.org/download/download?filename=5b761756-e6f3-4133-846a-b60e28f67ef4.pdf&urlslug=IAAF%2BCompetition%2BMedical%2BGuidelines%2B-%2BJanuary%2B2013%2BFirst%2BEdition); [World Athletics health document index](https://worldathletics.org/about-iaaf/documents/health-science).

**V:** NWS recommends stopping for thunder/lightning danger, substantial enclosed shelter or closed hard-top vehicles, and at least 30 minutes after last thunder before resuming. Trees and tents are not shelters. **A:** trigger action earlier using forecast/detection plus evacuation travel time; demonstrate enough reachable shelter for participants/staff/spectators. A road or trail cannot remain “go” because the village has a tent. Reopening needs incident-command clearance; keep medical/aid active through sweep and evacuation. [NWS outdoor sports lightning](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-sports).

## Common mistakes [A]
- Treating a picturesque lawn as permitted usable area, or designing over roots, slopes, wetlands or a construction closure.
- Calling Sloan's perimeter loop a5K: tourism source describes2.6 miles, about4.18km; an approximately0.82km added section still needs exact certified measurement. [VisitDenver SloanLake guide](https://visitdenver.com/blog/post/sloan-lake/).
- Putting a mass start directly before a narrow bridge/turn, or returning out-and-back runners through the same start wave.
- Closing a cycle lane informally, blocking public/accessible access, or letting toilet and bag queues cross finish runoff.
- Treating one accessible toilet as enough regardless of clusters, or relying on closed permanent restrooms.
- Hiding medical behind expo, dead-ending ambulances, or staffing only by participant count.
- Treating water consumption, crowd-flow coefficients, tent wind limits or heat flags as universal constants.
- Removing aid before the last runner, failing to reconcile starters/finishers/withdrawals, or reopening before the sweep.
- Confusing certification with permits, and concept art with an as-built safety plan.
