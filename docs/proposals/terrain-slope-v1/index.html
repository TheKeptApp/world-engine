Terrain + Slope

Rich Style B · calibration v2 · seven fictional blocks across three viewing distances.

Floors level. Foundations meet the footprint.

Grade belongs to terrain and streets. Buildings keep vertical walls and horizontal floor slabs. Terraces, entries and retaining walls resolve the difference.

Pictures are design references, not surveyed sites. Exact grade diagrams and machine-readable settings accompany every sheet. Complete 1 m coverage and several city maximum grades remain unverified.

Gentle block · 3%

Seattle

Low exposed foundations and shallow stoops. Grade remains subtle; avoid exaggerating terrain to announce the band.

Moderate block · 10%

San Francisco

Each house has its own horizontal floor datum. Lower garage openings and raised entries must connect to local sidewalk levels.

Steep block · 22%

Los Angeles hills

Terraces, split-level entries and lower garages reveal height difference without tilting houses. The 22% fixture is not a new-street compliance claim.

Steep corner lot

San Francisco

Sample both street frontages. Uphill entry, downhill garage and wrapping retaining walls require separate elevation constraints.

Stair street

Pittsburgh

Pedestrian stair route with flight/landing rhythm. Do not infer an accessible route from stairs or copy individual generated step dimensions.

Retaining-wall street

Kansas City

Limestone retaining edges and stair breaks make uphill yards readable; do not turn the entire street into a continuous cliff.

Canyon edge

San Diego

Keep natural ravine slope distinct from the road grade. Constrain pads and preserve canyon breaklines in every LOD.
