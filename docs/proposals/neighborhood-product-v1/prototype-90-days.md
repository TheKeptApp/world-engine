# 90-day prototype recommendation

**Build Common Ground as the lead hypothesis; use five Gather screens to test the Builder-to-social bridge.** The programme starts after a usable Builder foundation. This is an internal prototype, not a public launch. No outreach or recruitment is authorized by this deliverable.

## Days 1–15: define and de-risk

One curated urban public-space district, six short challenges, two mobility/browse alternatives, one shared weekly scene. Map/route curator documents public access and known gaps. Designer tests ten screens with internally approved adult testers. Engineer and privacy specialist define local storage, relay requests, anonymous contribution protocol, suppression and deletion. Use synthetic aggregates until this design passes adversarial review.

Exit: testers can explain that nobody sees their route, home or attendance; a public-place challenge is useful solo; dangerous/private targets removed. Proposed gate: at least 8/10 understand privacy unaided, and no severe disclosure in the threat review. These are small-sample decision rules, not statistical proof.

## Days 16–35: Common Ground vertical slice

Implement manual area selection, read-only 3D scene, daylight/weather source timestamp, challenge card, local completion and local collection. No GPS recording. Fit within the existing engine's verified phone budget; if that budget is not met, use a static calibration image preview with an honest prototype label. Add weather unavailable/severe-condition fallback, screen-reader labels, large-text support and equivalent seated/browse completion. Do not add messaging, payments, open submissions or gamified streaks.

Exit: end-to-end local flow works offline after asset caching; privacy packet/log audit sees no route, residence or persistent identifier. Check low-memory devices and time-to-interaction against the existing mobile engine baseline; no unverified numerical performance promise.

## Days 36–55: cooperative experiment

Internal adults, opt-in research, one weekly chapter. Use synthetic totals first; real anonymous contributions only after privacy review. Compare solo completion with the shared-scene condition in a small exploratory study. Collect an explicit weekly feedback form and minimal consented research records separate from product activity; never route telemetry. Proposed directional measures: voluntary return in week 2, completed challenge, perceived fun, privacy comprehension and accessibility completion. Recruitment and research consent require their own approved process.

Exit: a meaningful subset returns without reminders and names the shared goal/real place as a reason. Provisional gate: ≥40% week-2 voluntary return among ≥20 consenting internal testers and ≥70% of completers call the challenge worth repeating. Report denominator and dropouts. Small sample results are directional and not growth forecasts.

## Days 56–75: narrow Gather slice

One vetted public plaza, one small social-afternoon template, at most six static Builder objects, access corridor, private local save, two time/weather options and an invitation preview. No organizer marketplace, RSVPs, attendees, payments, actual invitations, street closures or claims of permitting. Observe whether adults can assemble and explain a viable draft without confusing it with approval.

Exit: ≥8/10 testers distinguish a rendered plan from real permission and preserve the access corridor unaided. If they treat layout validation as safety certification, change the UX before further tests.

## Days 76–90: decide

Repeat phone and privacy checks, test missing-weather/sparse-group states, review operating/content cost and interview for willingness to pay with transparent hypothetical offers. Produce a costed next-step backlog. No billing, external launch or live-location exception is a shortcut.

**Continue Common Ground** if voluntary repeat use, comprehension, accessibility and content cost meet the agreed gates with no material privacy issue. **Pivot toward Gather** if organizer utility is stronger and Builder is stable, accepting lower frequency. **Stop social features** if demand depends on people tracking, precise attendance, residential identity or public conflict. Keep the solo neighbourhood experience if it remains useful.

## Team / dependencies / non-goals

Proposed team: one product designer, one client engineer, shared WorldEngine engineer, part-time public-route/content curator and privacy/security specialist. Actual staffing, engine readiness and budget unverified. Weather/data licences, map correctness and local public-site facts are dependencies, not solved by the concept.

Do not build nationwide content, open chat, AR, leaderboards, friend maps, background location, automated route inference, address verification, payment services or public release in 90 days. Revenue ideas remain hypotheses; pricing is intentionally uncommitted.
