# Weather for your block — product logic
7 October 2026 · One-page proposal · Working name: Blocklight

**Promise.** See what the next few hours could feel like on your own street: changing shade, rain, snow, fog and evening light in a familiar 3D world. The geography is local; the weather remains an area estimate. The app does not claim a sensor at every house.

**Core loop.** Open to the saved block and a short current-weather summary. Read the source badge and last update. Drag the hour rail to a planned departure time; shadows and weather change while the house stays put. Switch street/aerial when it helps understand shade. Choose a time, then leave the app. Return when weather changes or tomorrow’s plan needs checking. Active official warnings interrupt this loop with readable text and an official link.

**Daily reason to open.** Morning: understand the commute or school-run conditions. Afternoon: compare a walk, garden task or outdoor meal across a few hours. Evening: check the next morning and watch the block move into night. Familiar surroundings make an abstract forecast easier to imagine; beauty supports the decision, rather than requiring a long session. Retention and usefulness are hypotheses to test, not established results.

**Free.** One locally saved home block; current estimates; a complete basic 24-hour scrub; rain/snow/fog/night rendering; supported official warnings and their notifications; all source labels, freshness, attribution, accessibility and privacy controls. Station observations appear only where an actual timestamped observation is available. Never paywall a warning, current safety information or data provenance.

**Paid.** A planning layer: extended playback up to the provider’s available horizon, side-by-side times, reusable local planning slots, additional locally saved places and optional widgets. Start with one subscription, no ads and no location brokerage. Test willingness to pay before choosing a price. Paid features extend planning convenience; free users still get the defining 3D block experience.

**Trust.** Observed means a measurement from a named station at a stated time, not from the house. Forecast means a provider estimate with issue and valid times, including WeatherKit’s current estimate. Simulated means rendered or computed appearance: shadows, puddles, snow cover and night windows. Every value carries its label. Stale records keep their original category, gain STALE and an age, and lose any “live” styling. Missing data stays unavailable. The concept pack’s sample values and warning are entirely fictional.

**Privacy.** The exact home coordinate, selected house and any local nickname remain on the device. Network requests use coarse area packages; matching the house and warning polygons happens locally. No account is needed for the core loop. V1 has no home-scene sharing. A coarse area request can still reveal an approximate area; do not market this as zero location disclosure.

**First test.** Prototype on a few opt-in public test blocks, with local selection. Ask whether people can explain the labels and choose a useful outdoor time within twenty seconds. Measure repeat opens and planning actions without addresses, coordinates or home imagery. Validate battery, low-data and offline use before extending the catalog.
