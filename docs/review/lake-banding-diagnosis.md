# 600 m lake bands: read-only diagnosis

**Finding: ordinary depth-buffer precision causes the broad green rectangles and regular lake stripes.** The bakeoff uses a fixed 0.2 m near plane for both street and aerial cameras, unlike the ordinary web viewer's existing height-based near-plane rule. At 600 m this cannot reliably separate the exported centimetre-spaced ground and water layers. No renderer, shader, material, mesh, exposure or shipping camera code was changed. This report proposes a fix; it does not implement or grade it.

## Mechanism and alternatives

- [Bakeoff main](../../web/bakeoff/main.js) constructs `PerspectiveCamera(config.camera.fov,1,.2,150000)` and never calls the ordinary viewer's adaptive near rule. [CameraRig.updateNear](../../web/src/camera.js) already documents this exact aerial precision problem and uses `min(500,max(0.1,0.25*camera.position.y))`.
- [SceneGenerator](../../Sources/WorldGen/SceneGenerator.swift) fills each chunk's base lawn at y=0, then overlays water at y=0.03; the shore strip is y=0.034. [Context surfaces](../../Sources/WorldGen/Context/ContextFeatures.swift) also use centimetre-scale layering. The live Sloan water vertices measure 0.0299999993–0.0340000018 m. Chunk-level base faces explain the rectangular lawn interruptions; quantized depth crossings across projected triangles explain the regular thin stripes. This is depth fighting between distinct, closely spaced surfaces, not a missing lake tessellation requirement.
- The installed three.js pass creates a default unsigned-integer depth texture; its WebGL backend maps this to `DEPTH_COMPONENT24`. The original frame ledger also records a DEPTH_COMPONENT24 target. For conventional perspective depth, one quantization step is approximately `z²(f−n)/(fn(2²⁴−1))` in view-axis metres. At z≈848.53 m (the centre ground ray for a 600 m eye pitched 45°), this is **0.21458 m with n=0.2**, versus **0.00028582 m with n=150**. A 3 cm vertical layer separation projects to about 0.02121 m along that ray; even the 4 mm shore separation projects to 0.00283 m. Increasing near resolves these layers without changing them. Values vary across the image; this is a local analytical estimate, not a per-pixel GPU-depth readback.
- Fragment high-float precision reports 23 mantissa bits. No shader precision qualifier or math was changed for the probe. The broad artifact disappears under a projection-only change, ruling out insufficient shader arithmetic precision as its necessary cause here.
- Water tessellation/topology, world coordinates, shoreline texture, reflection, wave/noise code and exposure stayed fixed. Thus mesh refinement, a new shoreline field or exposure pin is unnecessary to remove the observed rectangles/regular stripes. Fine noisy water highlights remain after the probe; the existing high-frequency normal noise may merit a separate anti-aliasing review, but was not isolated by this test and is **not** claimed fixed. No causal claim about that remaining texture is made.

## Smallest general fix — proposal only

Have the bakeoff's camera setup reuse the **existing generic height-based near-plane policy**, evaluated after camera placement and when camera height changes, then update its projection matrix. Under the current flat export datum this gives near=150 m at 600 m and keeps the existing far range. Do not hard-code a Sloan name, lake, altitude-specific material offset or capture-only override. A shared helper would prevent the ordinary viewer and bakeoff from diverging; an owner can choose whether extracting it is warranted. Terrain-aware future cameras should use clearance above their ground datum, not absolute sea-level elevation.
