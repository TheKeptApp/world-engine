# Blocklight hyperlocal weather — recommendation first
Research date: 7 October 2026. US-first, iPhone-first. Prices are USD before tax. Verified means supported by a cited primary source; it does not mean API-tested or independently accurate.

## Recommendation
Launch with **Apple WeatherKit for live conditions, ordinary forecasts and next-hour precipitation; NWS for official alerts; NOAA MRMS/NOHRSC/SNODAS for independently retained precipitation and snow history; local solar computation and WorldEngine geometry for shadows.** Keep Apple live responses separate from the retained ground-state pipeline. This is the strongest launch fit on price, iPhone integration and available features, **not a verified winner for forecast accuracy**. Apple documents the next-hour service and published quotas; government products supply reusable history. [WeatherKit](https://developer.apple.com/weatherkit/), [NWS API](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api), [MRMS](https://registry.opendata.aws/noaa-mrms-pds/), [SNODAS](https://nsidc.org/data/g02158/versions/1).

At **1M MAU**, retain that stack initially. The base model needs 18M combined WeatherKit requests/month, fitting the 20M tier at **$999.99/month**. Budget another **$1,500/month** for public-history ingestion, storage/delivery and operations: about **$2,508/month including annual membership amortization**, with an infrastructure-dependent planning range of **$1,758–$4,008/month**. For procurement, allow 15% request overhead:20.7M calls crosses the 20M quota and requires the 50M tier at$2,499.99, bringing the midpoint to **$4,008/month** (about$3,258–$5,508 with the infrastructure range). Running a nationwide proprietary radar nowcaster is not justified merely to avoid this API bill. Change providers only after measured quality, licensing and workload evidence.

WeatherKit cannot be the unrestricted archived weather database behind every WorldEngine app. Its terms restrict persistent/derived databases and original-form feeds. Keep persistent weather/ground history on government or appropriately licensed data; confirm the paid Blocklight presentation satisfies transformed value-added conditions. [Apple agreement, Attachment 8](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/).

## Monthly costs and assumptions
Users mean **monthly active users**, not installed accounts. Base:12 ten-minute sessions/MAU/month; one combined live/forecast/next-hour fetch at each open;25% of sessions additionally refresh at 5 and 10 minutes:
- Combined requests/MAU =12×(1+0.25×2)=18.
- No cross-user Apple cache discount is assumed. Current/hour/day/minute datasets can share a REST request; **one such request counting as one billable call must be verified in the usage dashboard**. Extra availability, attribution, retry, alert detail, widgets and push jobs are outside the 18-call allowance. [Bundled request shape](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10003/).
-30-day month; quotas aggregate across the developer membership. Paid Apple tiers replace the included quota; they are not quota plus 500k.

## Minute rain: capability versus accuracy
US options with documented minute precipitation output include WeatherKit(60 min), MinuteCast(120 min), Tomorrow field-dependent timelines, Xweather(60 min), Weatherbit(60 min), and OpenWeather(60 min). Optional Visual Crossing minute fields are documented, but dedicated radar-nowcast quality/coverage is unresolved. Open-Meteo 15 minute model output and NWS hourly forecasts do not satisfy this feature. MRMS requires a separate extrapolation/forecast implementation.

No current independent, matched US block-level onset-error benchmark was verified that ranks these vendors. A1 km grid and 1 minute output do not mean 1 minute arrival accuracy. Rain can form, decay, change speed, evaporate below radar, or fall with mixed phase. Denver virga and Chicago shallow/lake-effect snow need separate evaluation. Research supports probabilistic radar nowcasting while documenting remaining uncertainty, not universal vendor error bounds. [Radar/growth research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03854-z), [heavy precipitation research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06184-4), [NOAA virga](https://inside.nssl.noaa.gov/mrms/2018/03/when-radar-observed-precipitation-does-not-reach-the-ground/), [winter research](https://www.nssl.noaa.gov/research/winter/).

**Blocklight product policy, proposed:** say “Rain may arrive near your block around 12 min” plus intensity chart, per-minute probability when supplied, and forecast age. Only show “10–20 min” as a confidence interval after upstream or local calibration supports it; do not invent a range. Apple per-minute probability is the chance of precipitation in that minute, not the confidence that the arrival time is correct. Do not aggregate correlated minute probabilities as independent events. [Probability field](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/minuteweather/precipitationchance).

Build a licensed validation set before claiming “most accurate”: city, season/storm type,0–15/15–30/30–60 minute lead, precipitation threshold, source age and native cell. Measure misses/false alarms, onset absolute error, intensity error and probability reliability/Brier score; retain provenance and held-out events. Prefer owned sensors or legally reusable observations. AccuWeather's standard terms restrict evaluation/algorithmic uses, so obtain the necessary custom rights before including its API data in such tests.

[competitor-notes.md](competitor-notes.md) separates documented UI practices, marketing and missing evidence. Weather Strip is a useful model for distinguishing radar nowcasts from hourly forecasts. RainViewer app capability is not a public commercial API entitlement.

## Snow history, melt and ground state
For **last 24/48/72 hour snowfall**, ingest NOHRSC gridded analyses, keeping analysis valid time and revision version. Their current cadence differs by accumulation window and late reports revise up to 5 days. For **remaining pack/melt**, use SNODAS daily depth, SWE, snowpack temperature and runoff plus public hourly forcing. Cite the SNODAS DOI and subset/access date. All these have$0 supplier fees; processing is in the infrastructure allowance. [NOHRSC](https://www.nohrsc.noaa.gov/snowfall_v2/), [SNODAS citation](https://doi.org/10.7265/N5TB14TC).

Do not confuse snowfall liquid equivalent, fallen-snow depth, retained snow depth, or melt runoff. Depth loss includes settlement; runoff is not merely yesterday's snowfall minus today's depth. Recent Open-Meteo past_days are archived model forecasts rather than street observations. Tomorrow snow conversion/retention are vendor-defined. These are useful fallback inputs under their licence, not proof of a clear curb. [Open-Meteo variables](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs), [Tomorrow snow interpretation](https://support.tomorrow.io/hc/en-us/articles/43215043291028-Snow-Data-on-the-Tomorrow-io-Platform).

Maintain a public-data-derived reservoir per real upstream cell: rain input; evaporation/drainage; snow retained mass; snowfall age; thaw degree-hours; solar/shade exposure; soil state and provenance. Label rendered wet/dry/frozen states **estimated**. HRRR soil temperature is not pavement temperature, and air below 0°C does not establish road ice or lake ice. Salt, shade, drainage, traffic and plowing are separate inputs. Municipal clear/plow records or user reports are needed for actual displaced banks; absent input, state is unknown. [NOAA soil evaluation](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/53800).

Cold-start last 1–3 days from public analyses; a missing interval is unknown, not zero precipitation. Reconcile when revised snow/QPE arrives; avoid replaying visual accumulation twice. Keep melt/weather history out of Apple-derived persistent storage.

## Sun and shadows
Compute solar angles locally from coordinates/time; compute occlusion against WorldEngine buildings, terrain and tree geometry. Sunrise/sunset endpoints cannot tell whether a particular tree shades a particular pavement. Vendor cloud cover, direct/diffuse irradiance and radiation forecasts can modulate brightness/shadow softness; that is the useful extra data. Open-Meteo exposes radiation fields. NOAA's online calculator is no longer maintained; use published equations or a maintained correctly licensed implementation rather than the website as a runtime dependency. Check library/code licences independently of the astronomical formula, including NLR/NREL SPA download terms. Weather supplier cost for the geometry/solar calculation itself is$0. [Solar reference](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/calcdetails.html), [equations](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/solareqns.PDF), [SPA](https://midcdmz.nlr.gov/spa/).

**Freshness policy, proposed rather than vendor-mandated:** track issued/analysis time, valid time, expiry, fetched time and quality/coverage independently. Honor contract/Cache-Control/expiry first; a fresh fetch can contain old source data. Warn at>5 min source age for onset; suppress a precise countdown beyond 10 min or on unknown provenance/coverage. These thresholds require calibration against the chosen feed, whose update cadence may differ. Current conditions older than 60 min and daily snow older than 36 h get visible age/unknown flags; don't relabel stale values “live.” Advance a forecast curve in valid time; fetching it again is not a new observation. Preserve alerts through supplied expiration only; show canonical source when unavailable.

NWS rate limits are unpublished: back off on errors, cache by grid and use bulk public distribution at scale; no rotating identities or endpoint scraping to evade limits. Historical graphics, protected pricing and blocked pages were not bypassed.
