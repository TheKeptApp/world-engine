# WorldEngine mobile world rendering — research v1

**Research date: 2026-10-07. Proposal only; no engine code changed.**

Show more world by keeping a small detailed neighborhood, replacing distant blocks with coarse silhouettes, and streaming only the detail the camera can use. Start with bounded sun shadows and screen-size LOD, then spatial batching. Do not make the whole city one mesh or keep every far tree at street-level detail.

## Evidence and budget contract

- **User-provided baseline, not independently measured:** A16, RealityKit/Metal, 60 fps, fewer than 100 draws, fewer than 400,000 triangles, roughly 13–15 ms GPU with shadows. Later renderer: three.js. No WorldEngine executable, capture or device benchmark was inspected for this research.
- **Derived arithmetic:** 60 fps allows 16.67 ms per frame. A 13–15 ms GPU frame leaves approximately 1.67–3.67 ms nominal GPU headroom. CPU and GPU overlap; do not add their durations as if they execute serially. A cold frame under 16.67 ms is not proof of sustained performance.
- **Proposal:** define draw and triangle budgets explicitly. Record main-view draws/triangles and total pass submissions separately, including shadow/depth passes. Until clarified, use the conservative acceptance test of <100 total draw submissions and <400k pass-expanded submitted triangles; this may be stricter than the current counters. Instance count times mesh triangles counts as submitted geometry, even when one draw renders many instances.
- **Proposal:** seek 11–12 ms warm GPU time as a starting headroom goal, not a measured capability or revised promise. Confirm through representative A16 testing. Apple recommends thermal-aware performance work and pass-level analysis. [Metal optimization](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/606/?time=160); [Xcode Metal performance analysis](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/analyzing-the-performance-of-your-metal-app).
- **Verified** below means a primary source documents the mechanism or shipped feature. **Assumption / estimate** means a WorldEngine recommendation or predicted resource saving. No technique has a verified WorldEngine FPS gain. Gains overlap and must not be added together.

## Proposed world representation and selection

**Assumptions / starter parameters, not tuned limits:** partition near content into roughly 64–128 m spatial cells, with far proxies covering roughly 256–512 m clusters. Measure multiple sizes: the objective is useful culling and batching, not adherence to a fixed grid dimension. Partition instance groups by cell, material and LOD. Preserve mapped true-scale location and skyline height; reduced detail must not shrink buildings to fit a draw budget.

Use three content tiers: detailed near facades/trees, simplified midrange roofs/walls/crowns, and far block/skyline proxies. Street and aerial cameras use the same world coordinates but different selection priorities. Aerial must not promote every visible house to street detail. Replace a parent only when child assets are decoded, uploaded and ready; retain the parent on failure. Avoid prolonged parent/child overlap because it increases triangles and produces depth artifacts. This adapts documented hierarchical selection and HLOD principles. [Cesium Native selection algorithm](https://cesium.com/learn/cesium-native/ref-doc/selection-algorithm-details.html); [Unreal hierarchical LOD](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/hierarchical-level-of-detail-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US).

For an object/tile with geometric error e, camera-space depth z, vertical field of view f, and viewport height H pixels, use the approximate projected error **E = e × H / (2 × z × tan(f/2))**. For selection, use a conservative positive distance to its bounds, not a behind-camera center. This is an authored simplified projection rule; large bounds and near-plane intersections require conservative handling. Cesium documents screen-space-error selection; MSFS documents projected-size selection. [CesiumJS Cesium3DTileset](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/Cesium3DTileset.html); [MSFS 2024 LOD selection](https://docs.flightsimulator.com/msfs2024/flighting/models-and-textures/modeling/lods/lod-selection-system/).

**Proposed starting hysteresis:** refine above 2.5 px error and coarsen below 1.5 px, with a short dwell of about 0.3–0.5 s in ordinary motion. These are assumed tuning values. Override delays after a teleport or major FOV change. Prefer discrete switches where detail is subpixel; if crossfades are required, budget temporary double geometry and transparent fill. Test foliage through a full aerial orbit: a single vertical tree card is inadequate overhead. Use reduced opaque crowns first, then multi-angle cards only where measured cheaper.

Fetch priorities should be: visible near gaps, visible child refinement, current-route lookahead, then a small lateral turn margin. Predict from camera velocity/heading or the known walk-recap route; cancel obsolete work. Street walking, aerial rotation and teleporting need different lookahead policies. Frustum culling happens before requests as well as before drawing. Separate render-visible, prefetch-eligible and shadow-caster bounds. Async loading is available, but its declaration does not promise that decode/upload/attachment work never stalls a frame. [RealityKit async entity loading](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/entity/init(contentsof:withname:)).

## Shadows, time, trees and atmosphere

**Proposal:** keep genuine moving sun shadows near the user, preserve farther building silhouettes, and use cheap sun-independent ambient grounding for far trees/structures. Shadow caster selection must include offscreen objects whose shadows reach visible receivers. At low sun, that region is much larger than the camera frustum. Bound the projected receiver footprint and caster influence rather than simply switching off all offscreen casters.

For established RealityKit SDKs, use directional shadow projection and caster participation. Do not build a baseline around deprecated maximumDistance or assume arbitrary cascade split/resolution/cache controls. The online cascade API exists but needs deployment verification. In three.js, CSM is available; it increases coverage/quality by dividing range, but each cascade introduces work. Treat it as a measured optional quality tier. [RealityKit directional shadows](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/directionallightcomponent/shadow); [RealityKit dynamic shadow participation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/dynamiclightshadowcomponent); [RealityKit Cascades](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/directionallightcomponent/shadow/cascades-swift.struct); [three.js cascaded shadow maps](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/CSM.html).

Avoid baking one noon sun into the ground or tree cards: WorldEngine's live solar position and recap scrubbing would become visibly wrong. Bake sun-independent AO/ground contact first. Multi-key baked solar lighting or static-shadow caches require time-key selection, invalidation and additional memory; mark them later/high effort. A tiny blob/contact decal can ground a trunk or parked car, but is not a substitute for a tall building's directional shadow. Native lightmaps are newly documented and version-gated. [three.js shadows](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/shadows.html); [RealityKit LightmapComponent](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/lightmapcomponent).

Start sky with a gradient/dome, shared solar direction and inexpensive clouds. A small atmosphere LUT is optional. Amortize costly LUT/cloud generation only if profiling justifies having it. Distant haze should lower contrast and support far proxies; it must also change selection to save resources. Clear-weather aerial views still need credible skylines. Night windows can be emissive without creating a shadowed point light at every house; pool a small set of nearby actual lights and measure wet-road reflections/halos in the worst case. These are authored proposals informed by atmosphere and pass-cost sources. [Ghost of Tsushima atmosphere](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/jpatry_advances2021/index.html); [three.js fog](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/fog.html); [three.js shadows](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/shadows.html).

## Memory, launch, battery and heat

**Proposal:** maintain separate accounting for compressed source bytes, decoded CPU mesh buffers, GPU geometry, texture mips, LOD duplicates, shadow/render targets, in-flight loads and shared assets. Cache budgets must reserve room for the rest of the app and temporary decode peaks. Evict by priority and recent use, protect shared-resource references, and cancel old prefetch requests. No universal A16 safe memory ceiling was verified: physical RAM is not an app's jetsam allowance; available-process-memory is advisory and changes. Do not allocate up to its reported maximum. [Available process memory](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/os/os_proc_available_memory); [CesiumJS Cesium3DTileset](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/Cesium3DTileset.html); [Mapbox tile cache budget](https://docs.mapbox.com/ios/maps/api/latest/documentation/mapboxmaps/tilecachebudgetsize/).

Use texture compression where device support and the pipeline permit it; test decode/transcode latency and image quality. Geometry transfer compression shrinks download size but generally still produces decoded geometry buffers. On the web, GLTFLoader needs configured decoders and KTX2 format detection; resource disposal matters. Native RealityKit support for arbitrary glTF compression extensions is not assumed. [three.js glTF loading](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFLoader.html); [three.js KTX2Loader](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/KTX2Loader.html).

Launch with a small cached/proxy starting block, sky and camera, then refine. **Measure both first displayed frame and first navigable 3D world**; a quick placeholder is not proof of fast useful launch. Keep shader/decoder initialization and uploads bounded. Offline should retain available coarse content and show missing detail honestly. Apple's launch metric primarily captures reaching the first frame. [Xcode launch time](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/reducing-your-app-s-launch-time).

Use a quality ladder tied to measured load, thermal notifications and Low Power Mode: shrink shadow reach/caster complexity, coarsen far LOD, reduce nonessential effects and prefetch, then reduce resolution if supported. Recover gradually with hysteresis. **Proposal:** enter a reduced tier promptly at serious thermal state; use device results to decide whether fair requires an earlier step, and restore only after a stable cool interval. A 30 fps fallback may protect the device but must be reported as failing the 60 fps target. Minimize needless downloads and paused/offscreen updates. Pokémon GO's battery-saver display feature is a user-experience precedent, not proof that its renderer shuts down. [Foundation ProcessInfo](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/processinfo); [Metal optimization](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/606/?time=160); [Pokémon GO Battery Saver](https://niantic.helpshift.com/hc/en/6-pokemon-go/faq/2472-what-is-the-battery-saver-setting/?s=general).

## Verification plan and decision gates

This is a proposed experiment sequence, not completed testing:

1. Capture the current A16 build at actual phone resolution. Separate CPU work, GPU passes, shadow cost, bandwidth, main-view counts and all-pass counts. Confirm which counters the existing limits describe.
2. Run shadow on/off, reduced caster range, low-poly opaque tree, and material/cell-batch experiments independently. A draw reduction that raises geometry/fill cost is not automatically a win.
3. Replay fixed street walks, dense tree views, aerial zoom/orbit, rapid turns and teleport jumps. Add low sun, night rain, snow, clear long-distance visibility and fast recap time scrub.
4. Record cold/warm first-frame and useful-world launch, downloaded bytes, cache hits/waste, CPU/GPU peaks, p50/p95/p99 frame time and resident-memory plateau. Record device, OS, renderer, resolution, quality tier, build and ambient conditions.
5. Run a **20–30 minute sustained replay** on an A16 device with thermal/battery monitoring. This duration is an assumed starting test, not an Apple certification rule. Compare performance under warm conditions and pressure eviction, including poor network/offline.
6. Adopt a technique only when it increases useful coverage or reduces the actual bottleneck without breaking phone readability, live time/weather correctness, budget or stability. Do not sum gains across overlapping experiments.

For three.js, renderer counters are useful but verify what they include; GPU timers/backend features vary. For RealityKit, use Xcode/Metal captures rather than assuming a scene overlay counts every pass. [Xcode Metal performance analysis](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/analyzing-the-performance-of-your-metal-app); [Metal GPU memory bandwidth](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/measuring-the-gpus-use-of-memory-bandwidth?changes=_7); [Mapbox rendering performance evaluation](https://www.mapbox.com/blog/new-advanced-tools-for-map-rendering-performance-evaluation).
