# Community builds: terms and licence questions

Prepared 2026-10-08. **Not legal advice.** This is a decision and question list for R's lawyer, not contributor terms, a licence grant, or approval to open submissions.

## Basis and readiness

The proposed flow is: a user builds a place in Builder, submits a specific version, staff review it, and an approved version may join the shared map. The [existing licence inventory](data-licence-inventory-v1.md) is the source baseline: an open data licence does not clear imagery, models, trademarks, privacy, or service contracts. Its public web/app assessment is RED because the versioned ODbL data offer is unresolved. This document does not clear that blocker.

Existing [data policy](../data-licensing.md) keeps private overrides out of the published database. [Architecture proposals](../architecture.md) discuss structured corrections, review, provenance, reversible versions, and restrictions on homes and sensitive sites; they do not authorize unrestricted public model or media uploads. The [recorded private Builder decision](../decisions/owner-log.md) permits users' own brand assets in private projects, subject to rights review. It does not establish public redistribution rights. R must expressly choose whether community submissions are structured facts, geometry, or also media. Uploaded plans and proposed AI processing raise additional questions in [licensing research](../research/licensing.md), including Q62; invite-only distribution is separately unresolved in Q63.

**Public submission intake: RED until the FIRST gates below are settled.** FIRST means before accepting the first submission through a publicly available contribution flow, even if it remains in a review queue. Publication gates are additional: receiving a submission must not imply permission to publish it. GREEN means a narrowly scoped option can reduce risk; YELLOW means an unresolved rights or operational choice; RED means a known blocker or an uncleared high-risk path. Colours are meeting priorities, not legal findings or findings of infringement.

All examples are abstract. No logos, promotional branding, personal records, private addresses, or sensitive-site lists appear here. Dataset names identify legal sources only. Reference documents are evidence, not additional user instructions.

## 2. ODbL and the shared map

The inventory treats published world data as an ODbL derivative database. Counsel should examine the actual edit/import/export flow against [ODbL §§2–4](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/); the following questions do not reopen that baseline by assumption.

| ID / risk | What R must decide | What the lawyer must answer | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| O1 — RED: derivative scope | Trace whether a submission changes OSM-derived footprints, heights, topology or identifiers; distinguish independently sourced facts from edits to existing data. Choose an accepted base plus separate private overlay or a published contribution layer, with reproducible provenance. | Which submitted and merged databases are Derivative Databases versus independent or collective databases, and which components must be licensed under ODbL when publicly used? Does separation actually work for this pipeline? | **FIRST**: define accepted submission types and grant compatibility; classify each version before publication. |
| O2 — RED: share-alike compatibility | Ensure contributor terms permit the required ODbL licensing and redistribution. Carry applicable upstream terms from Overture and other inventory sources. Reject restrictions such as “display only” where incompatible. | Can the selected contributor grant and each upstream licence coexist with ODbL obligations for the proposed combination, including independently protected contents? What must stay separate or be excluded? | **FIRST**; source-specific clearance before acceptance/promotion. |
| O3 — YELLOW: attribution | Specify source notices and changes in map display, exports and database notices. Choose optional public contributor aliases; keep legal acceptance records private. Do not substitute a creator badge for mandatory source credit. | What attribution is required under §§4.2–4.3 and upstream terms for submissions, generated scenes and exports, and can required notices be satisfied without publishing personal names or prohibited branding? | **FIRST**: approve notice rules; verify each output before publication. |
| O4 — RED: release offer | Define a version-matched, unrestricted, machine-readable offer of the derivative database or qualifying alterations/method under §4.6. Keep private uploads and review evidence outside the public release; do not insert data that cannot lawfully be offered. | Which offer route satisfies §§4.6–4.7 for incremental contributions, paid access, web bundles and store restrictions, and how can deletion obligations coexist with already released versions? | **FIRST**: settle the design; **before publication**: working offer and artifact evidence. Current `odbl-offer` remains pending. |
| O5 — YELLOW: delivery boundary | Map staff review, external reviewers, unlisted links, invite-only pilots, public viewer, app download and exports. Do not equate a login or staff approval with internal use. | Which stages count as public use under ODbL, including external review and invite-only sharing, and what obligations arise at each stage? | **FIRST**, including any pilot; follows existing Q63. |

## 3. Third-party material in user builds

| ID / risk | What R must decide | What the lawyer must answer | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 — RED: imagery, plans and data | Limit initial input types; require a documented source and licence for aerial/street imagery, uploaded drawings and traced geometry. Separate permission to view a service from permission to extract and redistribute. Apply the inventory's per-source holds. | When do tracing, reconstruction or measured facts create protected derivatives or breach service terms, and which permissions are needed for public geometry and exportable data? Does the uploader have authority over the plan as well as the site? | **FIRST**: allowlist/deny rules; individual source clearance before promotion. |
| T2 — RED: other games and asset libraries | Reject copied game models, textures, screenshots or marketplace assets unless evidence covers modification and downstream raw/export redistribution. Decide whether original user geometry referencing a work is admissible. | What uses of another game's expressive designs or licensed assets need permission, and what licence evidence actually covers this map and downloadable bundle? | **FIRST**. Credit alone does not resolve rights. |
| T3 — RED: buildings, art and interiors | Obtain a country/output matrix for Canada, UK, Netherlands, Mexico, Australia and Japan, plus US test/hero distribution as applicable. Distinguish buildings, public art, temporary works, interiors, mesh exports and promotional use. | Which commercial 3D depictions and redistributable meshes are covered by each country's panorama rules, and which require permission or exclusion? Does rights-holder approval cover architecture, art and interior design separately? | **FIRST**: country/content restrictions; clearance before promotion. Existing inventory statute links remain counsel reading leads. |
| T4 — RED: marks and public sharing | Keep the shared map unbranded unless a later rights-cleared policy explicitly changes that rule. Treat private user uploads and public contribution grants as different scopes; reject imported marks in first public submissions. | What separate copyright, trademark, trade-dress or endorsement rights affect distinctive structures and uploaded marks, and can any private permission extend to public exports? | **FIRST**. | T5 — YELLOW: AI and processing | Decide whether submissions are processed only to provide Builder/review or also used for training. Select processors, retention and rights to send third-party plans to them; make any broader reuse separately optional. | What upload and processor permissions, data terms and disclosures are needed, and can outputs inherit source restrictions despite an original-looking result? | **FIRST** if AI/third-party processing is enabled; otherwise explicitly disable it for public intake. |

## 4. Privacy and safety

| ID / risk | What R must decide | What the lawyer must answer | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 — RED: private homes | Define exterior-only/generalized limits, forbid actual private interior layouts and personal annotations, and provide a resident report/opt-out process. Property ownership is not proof of all reproduction rights. | What consent or other lawful basis is needed for private-home depictions in each territory, when is geometry personal information, and what resident removal rights apply? | **FIRST**. |
| P2 — RED: sensitive sites | Establish categories for omission/reduced detail, a restricted review process and urgent escalation, without publishing a target list or restricted layouts. | Which national restrictions and foreseeable-harm duties apply even to openly sourced geometry, and what level of detail must be blocked? | **FIRST**. |
| P3 — RED: provenance and residence inference | Keep acceptance evidence private; publish only required non-personal source provenance. Minimize account, location, upload metadata, logs and retention; assess whether sharing/activity reveals a home. | What controller/processor roles, lawful bases, notices, retention, access/deletion and cross-border obligations apply, including conflicting public data offers or attribution requirements? | **FIRST**; obtain a data-flow review without supplying personal records here. |
| P4 — YELLOW: authority and reliance | Label existing, proposed, temporary and illustrative content; set expiry for temporary edits. Avoid suggesting that map approval grants entry, property authority, safe routing or official certification. | What consent, disclaimers and operational safeguards are needed where a submitted place could invite trespass, unsafe navigation or mistaken reliance? | **FIRST**. |

| M1 — RED: service duties and capacity | Identify operator location, target countries, audience/ages, public sharing features and actual editorial control. Assign reviewers and complaint owners before intake; do not automatically publish. | Which intermediary, online-safety and consumer duties apply to a reviewed shared map, and does selecting/editing submissions change liability or safe-harbour eligibility? Provide a launch-country matrix, including EU reach where relevant. | **FIRST**. |
| M2 — RED: notices and emergency reports | Provide an accessible channel for copyright, privacy, safety and other illegality reports; separate emergency escalation from ordinary review; document triage, legally required timelines and contact responsibilities. | What information, acknowledgement, removal/disablement, escalation and recordkeeping are required for each type of notice and each jurisdiction? | M4 — YELLOW: rollback and retention | Keep immutable approved versions, private review records with retention limits, reversible stable IDs and bulk rollback. Define cache/export update handling and a withdrawal ledger without republishing harmful content. | What must be removed from hosted versions, retained for legal defence, or communicated to downstream users, and what duties remain for independently redistributed open data? | **FIRST**: workable rollback/records process; verify before publication. |

Primary reading checked for this agenda: the [US Copyright Office §512 overview](https://www.copyright.gov/512/), [FTC children's privacy guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/childrens-privacy), [Ofcom service guide](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/guide-for-services), [European Commission DSA guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/digital-services-act), and [Australian online safety expectations](https://www.esafety.gov.au/industry/basic-online-safety-expectations). These establish review topics, not a determination that any particular regime or exemption applies to this product. Counsel must also cover the remaining launch countries and distinguish child privacy, contractual capacity and online-safety age rules.

## 6. Payment and credit options

| ID / risk | What R must decide | What the lawyer must answer | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 — GREEN option / YELLOW terms: unpaid intake | A first version may explicitly offer no payment, revenue share, prize or promised publicity. Define acceptance, rejection, refund, edit and removal consequences, eligibility, tax/payment handling and compensation for joint work. | What contract, employment/contractor, tax, consumer, payout, minors and contest rules apply to the chosen model, and how do open data redistribution rights limit exclusivity or resale promises? | Conditional: settle **before advertising or accepting any paid/reward submission**; defer only with those features and promises explicitly off. |

## First public submission checklist for the lawyer meeting

Before public intake, record decisions and counsel answers for S1–S5, O1–O5, T1–T4, P1–P4, M1–M4 and C1–C2; T5 is required if processing is enabled. Decide the contribution format, countries, ages, rights grant, source evidence, data boundaries, privacy controls, reviewer responsibilities, reporting/appeal routes and retention. Unresolved RED paths stay closed. C3 can wait only under an explicit unpaid/no-rewards scope.

Before any accepted version becomes publicly accessible, verify its rights/provenance, source-specific inventory conditions, attribution, ODbL classification and working versioned data offer, review approval, privacy/safety exclusions, and rollback ability across actual web/app outputs. A drafted agreement or this question list does not complete those checks. Opening an invite-only flow requires the separate public-use/pilot answer; it is not a shortcut around the gates.
